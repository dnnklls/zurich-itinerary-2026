Thursday 17 September

Day one: the easy one.

You lost a night on the aircraft. Sleep first, then one tram ride into the old town and back. Nothing that needs a ticket or a booking.

Morning

Land, and sleep it off.

In the room by about half nine. Close the curtains and take a real four-hour sleep — Zürich is six hours behind Singapore, so your body thinks it is mid-afternoon. Set an alarm for 13:30 or Thursday evaporates.

Evening

The lake at golden hour.Free

Bürkliplatz is where the river meets Lake Zürich. Walk the promenade east towards Zürichhorn — swans, the Alps on the far shore if the air is clear, and the sun going down behind the hills around 19:30. This is the picture everyone remembers of Zürich.

If your legs still work, the ZSG ticket office at Bürkliplatz sells the 1h30 short lake cruise from about CHF 9.40, and a Zürich day pass discounts it. Afternoon departures at 15:25 and 17:50.

Dinner in the old arsenal.

Friday 18 September

Day two: the drive.

Hire car from the airport, 137 km south over the Brünig Pass, and the Eiger standing over you by nine. One deadline governs the whole day — leave Grindelwald at 17:00.

Morning

Collect the car at six.Book tonight

The Car Rental Center sits on Level 1 of the Airport Center and opens at 06:00. Allow twenty to thirty minutes for paperwork — you want to be moving by quarter to seven.

Getting there at 05:40. Tram 10 or 12 from Glattpark, nine minutes, about CHF 4. First trams reach the airport around 04:45 so it should be running — but the hotel shuttle does not help, it only starts at 06:30. Simplest answer: book a taxi at reception tonight, roughly CHF 25 split between you, and stop worrying about it.

Three things decide whether this works. The driver named on the booking must present their own credit card — they hold CHF 1,000–3,000 as deposit and debit cards are refused. Bring the driving licence, and an IDP if anyone has one; a Singapore licence is in English so it should be accepted, but individual desks do sometimes insist. And confirm the motorway vignette is on the car — Swiss rentals include it, but check, because without one you are fined.

The drive down.

A4 south past Zug, A14 into Lucerne, A2 to Hergiswil, then the A8 over the Brünig Pass through Lungern and Brienz to Interlaken, and up the valley through Zweilütschinen. About 2h10 for 137 km. The Brünig stretch is the beautiful part and it is an ordinary road, not a serious mountain pass.

Park at Grindelwald Terminal — 1,032 spaces, open around the clock, and it is the base station of the Eiger Express, so you park and board lifts under one roof.

Late morning

The toboggan run at Pfingstegg.The slide

This is the one you wanted, and unlike everything within an hour of Zürich it actually runs on a weekday. Cable car up from the village, then a proper Rodelbahn down the mountainside with the Eiger north face opposite. Open 09:00–18:00 daily, season runs to 25 October.

Around CHF 6 a ride with the cable car on top. You cannot reserve a slot — turn up and queue. It closes in bad weather, so if the morning is wet, ring ahead before committing the drive.

Afternoon

Up to First.

Twenty-five minutes on the gondola from the village. The Cliff Walk is a twenty-minute walkway bolted to the rock face, included in the lift ticket, staring straight at the Eiger. From the top station it is an hour's easy walk to Bachalpsee, the lake that mirrors the Schreckhorn — the best hour in the Oberland if the weather holds.

Last gondola down is around 17:30, but that is later than you can afford. Be off the mountain by 16:30.

Don't attempt Jungfraujoch.

It is the famous one, and it is the wrong call today. Expensive, and it eats the entire day — you would get up there and immediately have to come down. Pfingstegg and First give you more mountain for the hours you actually have.

Save it for a longer trip

Wheels rolling out of Grindelwald at 17:00 puts you at the airport around 19:30 and in bed by eleven. 18:00 is the absolute latest. Every twenty minutes you linger comes straight out of the sleep before an 08:45 call.

If things change

Plan B, C, and rain.

The plan above assumes decent weather and a body that cooperates. When neither is true, drop down a level — guilt-free.

If it's pouring

The toboggan run and the mountain views both die in bad weather. Check before you commit 2h10 of driving.

  • Ring Pfingstegg before you leave — the run closes when wet.
  • Low cloud means First shows you nothing. Don't pay for the gondola.
  • If the Oberland is socked in, cancel the car and take the train to Rapperswil instead.
  • Or stay in Zürich: Kunsthaus, Landesmuseum, covered Bahnhofstrasse.

If you're tired (Plan B)

Four hours twenty behind the wheel is a lot two days off a red-eye. There is no shame in binning the car.

  • Don't drive tired. If the nominated driver is wrecked, someone else drives or nobody goes.
  • Train to Rapperswil instead — 35 minutes, lake town, castle.
  • Come back across the lake by boat and sit down for the whole hour.
  • Early dinner, home on tram 10 by nine.

If you're completely destroyed (Plan C)

Some rotations just win. Protect the sleep and the flight home.

  • Do not leave Glattpark.
  • Hotel gym and health club are free to crew; the crew lounge runs 24 hours.
  • Room service until 23:00, 20% off with crew ID.
  • Walk the Glattpark lake behind the hotel and call it a day.

Before you land

Logistics.

Phone and apps

Nothing is blocked — Google Maps, WhatsApp and Instagram all work normally. Switzerland is not in the EU, so EU roaming bundles do not cover it: check your plan or buy an eSIM. Get ZVV for tram tickets and SBB Mobile for trains.

Paying for things

Swiss francs, and cards work everywhere including trams and ticket machines. Zürich is expensive — a plain lunch is CHF 25–30, a beer CHF 8. Your allowance goes further at the hotel with the 20% crew discount. Duty free back in is CHF 300 of goods.

September weather

Around 21°C by day and 13°C after dark — the drop at sunset is bigger than it sounds. Grindelwald sits at 1,034 m and First at 2,168 m, so it will be meaningfully colder up there — take a proper jacket, not a shirt. Roughly one day in three sees rain.

Getting around

Tram 10 at the door does everything: hotel to city in 30 minutes, hotel to airport in 9. A 3-zone 24-hour ticket is CHF 14.40 and covers trams, buses and S-Bahn. Buy before you board — inspectors fine on the spot. Trams stop around midnight.

Driving in Switzerland

Motorways need a vignette, CHF 40 — rentals include it, but confirm at the desk. Limits are 120 on motorways, 80 on open road, 50 in towns, and the cameras are everywhere and unforgiving. Headlights on at all times, day or night. Blood-alcohol limit is 0.05%, but for you it is zero — you are operating in the morning.

Watch out for

Per station info: pickpockets around the main station and the airport. Avoid Platzspitz park and the ground behind the Landesmuseum, and Langstrasse in Kreis 5 after dark. And the 10-hour alcohol rule before duty is real — random blood tests happen at European stations.

Crew perks at the Kameha

Buffet breakfast CHF 19, business lunch CHF 24 (book it that morning), 20% off all food and drink including room service, 30% off laundry, free health club and gym, wifi on up to three devices, and a 24-hour crew lounge with a microwave. Room service 06:00–23:00. Reception handles wake-up calls. Strictly non-smoking — CHF 400 penalty.
