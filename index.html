Thursday 17 September

Day one: the easy one.

You lost a night on the aircraft. Sleep first, then one tram ride into the old town and back. Nothing that needs a ticket or a booking.

Morning

Land, and sleep it off.

In the room by about half nine. Close the curtains and take a real four-hour sleep — Zürich is six hours behind Singapore, so your body thinks it is mid-afternoon. Set an alarm for 13:30 or Thursday evaporates.

Evening

The lake at golden hour.Free

Bürkliplatz is where the river meets Lake Zürich. Walk the promenade east towards Zürichhorn — swans, the Alps on the far shore if the air is clear, and the sun going down behind the hills around 19:30. This is the picture everyone remembers of Zürich.

If your legs still work, the ZSG ticket office at Bürkliplatz sells the 1h30 short lake cruise from about CHF 9.40, and a Zürich day pass discounts it. Afternoon departures at 15:25 and 17:50.

Dinner in the old arsenal.

The Zeughauskeller is a 1487 armoury turned beer hall — long shared tables, halberds on the walls, and the Swiss things you actually came for: rösti, sausages, Zürcher Geschnetzeltes. Loud, unpretentious, walk-in friendly if you go early. Budget CHF 30–45.

Friday 18 September

Day two: the real one.

One long loop. North to the waterfall in the morning, back through the old town, up a hill for sunset. Every leg starts from a station you are already standing in.

Morning

Rhine Falls, before the crowds.Half day

Europe's largest waterfall, and it is closer than you think. Take tram 10 a few stops to Zürich Oerlikon — no need to go into the main station — then the direct train to Neuhausen Rheinfall, about 42 minutes, every half hour. Leave the hotel around 08:00 and you are standing over the water by half nine, ahead of the coach tours.

Walk the Schloss Laufen side: the viewing platforms drop you down the cliff until you are almost in the spray. The Känzeli platform is the one worth queueing for. Castle grounds and platforms about CHF 5.

Afternoon

Climb the Grossmünster.Friday only

Ride the train back to Zürich HB — you will be in the centre by about two. The Karlsturm tower of the Grossmünster is 187 steps and opens onto the best rooftop view in the city: the old town, the river, the lake, the hills behind.

Check this before you walk over. Since August the tower only opens Friday to Sunday — Friday is your one shot — and the church is under renovation through 2026–27, so parts can close at short notice. Last entry around 16:30. Church is free; tower CHF 5, tickets on the door.

Evening

Uetliberg for the sunset.Drop it if you're spent

Zürich's own mountain, and you reach it from platform 22 inside the main station. The S10 climbs through the woods in twenty minutes; ten minutes uphill on foot from the top station puts the whole lake and the Alps below you. Catch the 18:15 or thereabouts and the light goes gold at 19:30.

Honest note: after the Rhine Falls this is a long day. If your legs have gone, skip it — the lake promenade does the same job at a tenth of the effort.

If things change

Plan B, C, and rain.

The plan above assumes decent weather and a body that cooperates. When neither is true, drop down a level — guilt-free.

If it's pouring

September in Zürich rains about one day in three. It does not stop the city.

  • Drop Uetliberg — the whole point of it is the view.
  • Rhine Falls still works in rain. The falls do not care.
  • Swap the hill for the Kunsthaus, or the Landesmuseum by the station.
  • Bahnhofstrasse and the old town lanes have long covered stretches.

If you're tired (Plan B)

The night flight hit harder than expected, but you still want a day.

  • Cut the Rhine Falls. Sleep in, go into town at noon instead.
  • Lindenhof, Niederdorf, Bürkliplatz — the whole thing is one flat walk.
  • Take the 1h30 lake cruise and sit down for all of it.
  • Early dinner, home on tram 10 by nine.

If you're completely destroyed (Plan C)

Some rotations just win. Protect the sleep and the flight home.

  • Do not leave Glattpark.
  • Hotel gym and health club are free to crew; the crew lounge runs 24 hours.
  • Room service until 23:00, 20% off with crew ID.
  • Walk the Glattpark lake behind the hotel and call it a day.

Before you land

Logistics.

Phone and apps

Nothing is blocked — Google Maps, WhatsApp and Instagram all work normally. Switzerland is not in the EU, so EU roaming bundles do not cover it: check your plan or buy an eSIM. Get ZVV for tram tickets and SBB Mobile for trains.

Paying for things

Swiss francs, and cards work everywhere including trams and ticket machines. Zürich is expensive — a plain lunch is CHF 25–30, a beer CHF 8. Your allowance goes further at the hotel with the 20% crew discount. Duty free back in is CHF 300 of goods.

September weather

Around 21°C by day and 13°C after dark — the drop at sunset is bigger than it sounds, especially on Uetliberg. Pack a light jacket and a compact umbrella. Roughly one day in three sees rain.

Getting around

Tram 10 at the door does everything: hotel to city in 30 minutes, hotel to airport in 9. A 3-zone 24-hour ticket is CHF 14.40 and covers trams, buses, S-Bahn and the Uetliberg train. Buy before you board — inspectors fine on the spot. Trams stop around midnight.

Watch out for

Per station info: pickpockets around the main station and the airport. Avoid Platzspitz park and the ground behind the Landesmuseum, and Langstrasse in Kreis 5 after dark. And the 10-hour alcohol rule before duty is real — random blood tests happen at European stations.

Crew perks at the Kameha

Buffet breakfast CHF 19, business lunch CHF 24 (book it that morning), 20% off all food and drink including room service, 30% off laundry, free health club and gym, wifi on up to three devices, and a 24-hour crew lounge with a microwave. Room service 06:00–23:00. Reception handles wake-up calls. Strictly non-smoking — CHF 400 penalty.
